Claims and proofs on Rholo

R-Gen

Rholang contracts and Lean 4 proofs for the CoEngineers and Ingenesist vision: engineering work becomes a claim, validation is its quality, and the pair is a K-Asset that keeps its qualifications, adjudication, and provenance.

What
A seven-term Rholang core for engineering claims: identity and credentials, a per-claim cell that creates a K-Asset on validation, a value-proposition cell, a single-adjudicator oracle, and per-asset provenance. Sixteen components, ten state cells, acyclic. One writer per entity; independent entities share no mutable state.
Why
A claim without validation is an assertion; validation without a claim has nothing to measure. Splitting work, credentials, payment, and disputes across separate systems loses the link between the work and the judgement about it. Pairing quantity with quality makes the record durable; attaching qualification, adjudication, and provenance keeps it with the asset.
The combination
Paper-derived primitives, object capabilities on Rholo, per-entity linear cells, and a Lean 4 model with a 32-pair race matrix. The core stops at approval and decision facts; pricing, payments, risk, panels, governance, handles, metrics, and the client consume them from outside.
Role
Contracts, Lean 4 model, paper mapping, specification; sole author
Period

Method

Specify the failure mode before the architecture; prove what can be proved; contain what cannot; record provenance with every result. Where proof stops is stated in the limits.

1The problem

The premise is a sentence from the CoEngineers and Ingenesist papers: “The fundamental problem is that the value of engineering is invisible.” Dan Robles and colleagues wrote in 2017 that there is little accounting of true engineering value because there are few tools that measure it. The papers propose a handshake: a claim states the work, definition, quantity, and criteria; a validation states quality and carries evidence; approval marks acceptable completion. The pair is a K-Asset.

Software splits that handshake across a tracker, a credential registry, a bank, email, and a folder. Each boundary loses the link between the work and the judgement about it. The asset cannot say who validated it, under what credential, against which definition, or what happened when it was disputed.

R-Gen puts the handshake on chain and keeps the record with the asset. Qualification, adjudication, and provenance are cells the claim participates in.

2The core

Seven deploy terms. Each primitive passes a removal test: if the remaining set can still express claim, validation, value proposition, and K-Asset, the removed primitive was not core.

  • Root. GenRoot, an installer-gated client directory; wired at install, never resolved at runtime.
  • Identity. Per-address identity cell; first-writer-wins directory.
  • Credentials. Genesis Issuer, per-credential cell, issued registry; proof of subject, scope, and active state.
  • Claim. Per-claim cell: work element, selection criteria, quantity and definition, clock, comments, value-proposition reference. Validation creates the K-Asset; the K-Asset library is private.
  • Value proposition. Per-proposition cell: propose, validate, comment, withdraw, dispute.
  • Oracle. Per-case cell: one credential-proved adjudicator, evidence, decision, execution through the subject’s private applyDecision capability.
  • Provenance. Per-asset anchors, comments, verify.

Sixteen catalogue components, ten state cells, acyclic. The core mints and transfers nothing and has no R-Cash or ERTP dependency. KAT, pricing, payments, risk, panels, governance, handles, metrics, and the web client are extensions that consume approval and decision facts; the core never calls them.

3Paper to code

The mapping is literal. Each primitive page carries the paper quote and page, the Rholang arm, the Lean theorem, and the suite or corpus row that exercises it. The row-by-row matrix is docs/src/reference/paper-to-code.md; code headers repeat the citation keys and page numbers. A reader can start at a sentence in Robles & Layton and end at a theorem name.

The Pareto ledger is a design test, not a claim about the papers. “Pareto” does not appear in the seven papers; the word is the author’s framing. The ledger labels each primitive paper-derived, paper-adjacent, a user decision, or engineering infrastructure, and records what was removed and why.

The papers are third-party copyrighted reference material, indexed at docs/papers/README.md and not covered by the AGPL. The contracts, model, and book are original work. The attribution is specific: Dan Robles’ CoEngineers and Ingenesist Project. The repository is r-gen; the Lean namespace is RGen because Lean identifiers cannot contain a hyphen.

4Concurrency and proof

Every entity is a factory-created linear cell with one writer. A mutating arm consumes the state first, evaluates every guard on the post-consume state, and re-posts exactly once on every path. A second operation on the same entity sees the new state and refuses. Two operations cannot both observe the same pre-state and both commit.

Independent entities share no mutable cell. Identity creation and credential issuance are the only serialization points, neither on the claim/validation hot path. Oracle cases receive the subject’s private applyDecision capability in closure, so a decision cannot be applied to another K-Asset.

The Lean model states the general result: RGen.core_races_not_representable says no two events on one cell in any run share an expected generation; RGen.core_arm_authority says every arm’s decision factors through its consumed cell.

5Evidence

On 4 November 2026, against Rholo node e2d073d, the seven-term core installed on a fresh isolated devnet. Ten behavioural suites passed: identity, credentials, claim, K-Asset, value, oracle, provenance, integration, the core vertical slice, and the race regression. They printed 291 ok assertions.

The Lean project builds 36 files without Mathlib. The audit covers 131 public theorems and reports only propext and Quot.sound; no Classical.choice appears in the audited set, and there is no sorry, admit, native_decide, or declared axiom. The flow manifest maps 66 arms: 35 proved-model, 0 proved-conformance, 31 owed. The model emits 21 corpus rows; 35 mutating arms are tied to a consumed cell. The adversarial review records four P0 defects in the legacy 12-term prototype and their removal.

6Limits

  • Maturity. Devnet-only, unaudited; no real value. There is no token sale, pricing, transfer, or ERTP in the core.
  • Extensions absent. Receipt/KAT, payments/R-Cash, risk/SPE, panels, governance, handles, metrics, and the web client are not implemented in this tree.
  • Proof boundary. Lean proves the model, not a mechanized Rholang refinement. The arm map is hand-transcribed; proved-conformance is zero and 31 arms are owed.
  • Concurrency wording. “Maximal concurrency” means no shared mutable cell across independent entities and one writer per entity. It is not an absolute optimality theorem. Identity creation and credential issuance serialize deliberately.
  • Oracle. Selection is deterministic and named by the parties; it is not a random beacon.
  • Attribution. The seven bundled papers are third-party copyrighted works, not AGPL. The repository is AGPL-3.0-only.
  • Scope. The core records approval and decision facts. It does not price, pay, score risk, run panels, or administer governance.
