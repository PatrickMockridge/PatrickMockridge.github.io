Engineering records on Rholo

R-Gen

Engineering judgement is scattered across trackers, credentials, email, banks, and folders. R-Gen puts a claim and its validation on one chain, so the record travels with the asset.

I invested in CoEngineers in 2017 and have been involved since. R-Gen is my continuation of that thread: Dan Robles’ CoEngineers and Ingenesist vision, now expressed as smart contracts in Rholang, Rholo’s contract language, and a model in Lean 4, a proof assistant. The papers begin with a plain observation — the value of engineering is invisible because almost nothing records the judgement about the work — and this is a small, working attempt to record it.

What
A small Rholang core for engineering claims. A claim records what was done: the work, definition, quantity, and criteria. A validation records how well, and who was qualified to say so. Approval turns the pair into a K-Asset — a knowledge asset that carries its qualifications, adjudication, and provenance. Seven deploy terms, sixteen catalogue components, ten state cells, acyclic.
Why
A claim without validation is an assertion. Validation without a claim has nothing to measure. Today the work sits in a tracker, the credential in a registry, the payment in a bank, the argument in email, and the evidence in a folder. Each boundary breaks the link between the work and the judgement about it. The asset cannot say who validated it, under what credential, against which definition, or what happened when it was disputed.
The combination
Paper-derived primitives, object capabilities on Rholo (unforgeable references that grant exactly the authority they name), one linear cell per entity (a single-writer piece of state), and a Lean 4 model with a proved race-freedom result over the model and a 32-pair race matrix. The core stops at approval and decision facts; pricing, payments, ERTP (Rholo’s token standard), risk scoring, panels, governance, and the client consume those facts from outside.

Method

Specify the failure mode before the architecture; prove what can be proved; contain what cannot; record provenance with every result. Where proof stops is stated in the limits.

1Why engineering value is invisible

The papers start with a blunt sentence: “The fundamental problem is that the value of engineering is invisible.” Dan Robles and colleagues wrote in 2017 that there is little accounting of true engineering value because there are few tools that measure it. Their remedy is a handshake. A claim states the work, definition, quantity, and criteria; a validation states quality and carries evidence; approval marks acceptable completion. The pair is a K-Asset.

A load calculation makes it concrete. The calculation is the claim; a second engineer checks the assumptions and signs it, and their credential is what makes the signature mean something. Today the calculation is in a folder, the check in an email, and the fee in an invoice. Months later the record cannot say who checked it, against which version, or what happened in a dispute.

R-Gen puts that handshake on chain and keeps the record with the asset. Qualification, adjudication, and provenance are cells the claim participates in.

2The small core

Seven deploy terms cover the handshake: root, identity, credentials, claim, value proposition, oracle, and provenance. The claim holds the work, criteria, quantity and definition, clock, comments, and value-proposition reference; validation creates the K-Asset, and the asset library stays private. The oracle holds one credential-proved adjudicator and the evidence, then executes through the subject’s private applyDecision capability, so a decision cannot be applied to another K-Asset.

The removal test is the design rule: take a primitive out; if the rest can still express a claim, a validation, a value proposition, and a K-Asset, it was not core. That leaves seven deploy terms, sixteen catalogue components, and ten state cells, with no cycles. The core mints and transfers nothing and has no R-Cash or ERTP dependency. Pricing, payments, risk scoring, panels, governance, handles, metrics, and the client are extensions that consume approval and decision facts; the core never calls them.

3From paper to theorem

The lineage is only worth claiming if it can be checked. Each primitive page carries the paper quote and page, the Rholang arm, the Lean theorem, and the suite or corpus row that exercises it; the row-by-row matrix is docs/src/reference/paper-to-code.md. A reader can start at a sentence in Robles & Layton and end at a theorem name.

The Pareto ledger is a design test, not a claim about the papers. “Pareto” does not appear in the seven papers; it is the author’s framing. The ledger labels each primitive paper-derived, paper-adjacent, a user decision, or engineering infrastructure, and records removals.

The papers are third-party copyrighted reference material, indexed at docs/papers/README.md and not covered by the AGPL. The contracts, model, and book are original work. The attribution is specific: Dan Robles’ CoEngineers and Ingenesist Project. The repository is r-gen; the Lean namespace is RGen because Lean identifiers cannot contain a hyphen.

4Concurrency and proof

Every entity is a linear cell: state that a contract consumes, checks, and puts back exactly once, like a baton passed by hand rather than a whiteboard everyone can write on. A mutating arm consumes first, evaluates every guard against what it consumed, and re-posts exactly once. A second operation sees the new state and refuses. No two operations can both observe the same pre-state and both commit.

Independent entities share no mutable cell. Identity creation and credential issuance are the only serialization points, neither on the claim/validation hot path. Oracle cases receive the subject’s private applyDecision capability in their closure, so a decision cannot be applied to another K-Asset.

The Lean model states the general result. RGen.core_races_not_representable says no two events on one cell in any run expect the same version; RGen.core_arm_authority says every arm’s decision comes from the cell it consumed. The proof is over the model, not the deployed Rholang.

The 32-pair matrix is the boundary of that claim. Nineteen pairs are exclusive: whichever runs first, the second refuses. Twelve are ordered: one direction refuses, the other is the legal serialized order. One is independent. Every pair names a Lean theorem in spec/flows/races.yaml; tools/check_race_matrix.py checks the class vocabulary and that each declaration exists.

5Evidence

On 4 November 2026, against Rholo node e2d073d, the seven-term core installed on a fresh isolated devnet. Ten behavioural suites passed, from identity and credentials through claim, K-Asset, value, oracle, provenance, integration, a core vertical slice, and the race regression, printing 291 ok assertions.

The Lean project builds 36 files without Mathlib. The audit covers 131 public theorems and reports only propext and Quot.sound; there is no sorry, admit, native_decide, or declared axiom. The flow manifest maps 66 arms: 35 proved-model, 0 proved-conformance, 31 owed. Proved-model means the theorem is about the model; proved-conformance would tie a deployed arm to an emitted row and a passing suite, and none is claimed. The model emits 21 corpus rows. The adversarial review records four P0 defects in the legacy 12-term prototype and their removal.

6Limits

  • Maturity. Devnet-only, unaudited; no real value. There is no token sale, pricing, transfer, or ERTP in the core.
  • Extensions. Receipts, pricing, R-Cash payments, risk scoring, panels, governance, handles, metrics, and the web client are not implemented in this tree, and their suites are not implemented.
  • Proof boundary. Lean proves the model, not a mechanized Rholang refinement. The arm map is hand-transcribed; proved-conformance is zero and 31 arms are owed.
  • Concurrency. The precise claim is no shared mutable cell across independent entities and one writer per entity. It is not an absolute optimality theorem. Identity creation and credential issuance serialize deliberately.
  • Oracle. Selection is deterministic and named by the parties; it is not a random beacon.
  • Attribution. The seven bundled papers are third-party copyrighted works, not AGPL. The repository is AGPL-3.0-only.
  • Scope. The core records approval and decision facts. It does not price, pay, score risk, run panels, or administer governance.
