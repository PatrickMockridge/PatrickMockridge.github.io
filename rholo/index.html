Model and node

Rholo

A Rust node for the ρ-calculus: native object capabilities, horizontal scale-out by design, and laws proved at the model and tied to the node.

What
Rholo is a Rust implementation of the former RChain node: Rholang and RSpace, Casper consensus, storage, networking, and OCapN, ported crate by crate from the preserved Scala reference.
Why
The common pattern puts concurrency in the runtime and authority in a permission check. RChain made both structural: processes compose with |, and an unforgeable new name is the authority. The port keeps that concurrent, replicated state machine running and tests its laws against a machine; the organisation dissolved in 2022.
The combination
A ρ-calculus language where authority is a name and concurrency is native; a Rust node designed to shard horizontally; and a law register that ties machine-checked models to the running node by conformance corpora.
Role
Scala-to-Rust port of the former RChain node; law-first conformance and agentic assurance
Period
2026; port started August 2026; community testnet October 2026
Status
Pre-release; a community testnet is live at testnet.rhobot.net/status; the project calls itself pre-testnet because no stable testnet is declared; no production release.

Method

Specify the failure mode before the architecture; prove what can be proved; contain what cannot; record provenance with every result. Where proof stops is stated in the limits.

2Design

  • A name is a quoted process. Reflection is primitive: @P names a process and *x recovers it. Bundles are read/write facets, forwarders attenuate, and revocation is a language pattern. Authority is a name, not a permission table.
  • The law register is the oracle. Corpora emitted from the register run 1:1 through the node. The Scala reference contributes vectors, not truth.
  • Provenance is fixed before the port. Upstream is pinned and restorable, and its 165-file differential corpus is the reference’s surviving authority — as data, not specification.
  • Assurance is written and gated. Findings have one home and close by construction; HAZOP, bow-tie, a bounded-adversary tabletop, fuzzing, and a coverage floor leave a re-runnable record; it is not independent review.

3Provenance and rescue

Original: the Rust node, the Lean laws, the conformance corpora, and the assurance record. Inherited: the former RChain protocol and the upstream Scala, pinned at 1b7583649 and restorable with git checkout 1b7583649 -- legacy. 5Limits

  • No external audit firm. The security record is a written, gated self-adversarial programme: the September 2026 pass found no P0 (chain split, fund loss, or remote code execution); three P1s, four P2s, and fourteen P3s were confirmed and closed. The review ledger still carries 507 deferred rows of 626, and the September pass read the T1 roster, not the whole tree.
  • Formal assurance is layered. 72 of 77 laws are proved at all — 19 laws (23 entries) tied to the running node by conformance corpora, 53 laws (69 entries) over the model. Two of the 72 are vacuous, leaving 70 non-vacuous. Five laws are not counted as proved: law 19 is axiomatized by design, law 63a is open, and three are retired or orphaned. The Coq track defines the α-equivalence metatheory but states its laws as axioms.
  • Memory safety is first-party only. No unsafe covers the fourteen crates, not their dependencies; it rules out the memory-unsafety class, not leaks, logic errors, denial of service, or consensus bugs.
  • Sizing is an estimate. The node is storage- and uptime-bound, not CPU-bound; RAM and start-up time scale with chain length. The hardware page carries measured start-up figures, not a production benchmark.
  • Throughput is bounded and residency is superlinear. A block carries at most 255 deploys; the scaling page derives roughly 60–160 deploys/s on one shard and 6–20 s finality. Residency is Θ(N²): at a two-second block interval, 16 GB of memory is roughly twelve hours away, and sharding is untested at scale.
