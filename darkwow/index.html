Private bearer note

DarkWow

A private bearer-note system: the note is the asset, possession is authority, and no protocol-level administrator can freeze or confiscate it.

What
DarkWow is a Layer 1 design for private DeFi and private organisations. Its base asset is a bearer note: a commitment held by whoever knows its spend secret. The issuer may redeem, refuse, or dilute; the holder prices that as counterparty risk. What the protocol does not provide is a freeze list, an allowlist, or an admin key over the note.
Why
A private chain usually asks members to trust an operator; a public chain exposes membership and strategy. DarkWow tries to get both without a protocol-level administrator: authority is a name you hold, not an identity you assert; supply is checked at the block boundary; and the assurance boundary is written down. I could see that the basis of what DarkFi had could achieve this, but DarkFi had no interest in doing so.
The combination
Rholang/RChain (names as authority) + Halo 2 (private state transitions) + a manifest-driven capability wallet + a per-block Pedersen supply audit.

1The thesis

The note is the asset. The commitment is the note; the nullifier secret is possession. To transfer or redeem, the holder proves knowledge of the secret and publishes a nullifier; no entrypoint can move the note without it. There is no freeze opcode, no allowlist, and no admin key in the Promissory Note contract.

No turnstile at the protocol layer. The native token and the note do not require registration. Identity credentials are opt-in; issuer contracts and relayers are explicit counterparties. The protocol does not gate the base asset.

The issuer is the counterparty. PN does not enforce redemption, collateral, or a supply cap. RedeemV1 burns a note and creates a zero-value receipt; releasing the underlying asset is the issuer’s act. The issuer may also dilute. The holder prices both. The absence of enforcement is the design.

That is the difference from an ACL or membership system: there is no turnstile to capture. What remains is counterparty risk, stated and priced, not hidden behind an administrator.

2How the system composes

  • Promissory Note is the base asset. RegisterTypeV1, IssueV1, TransferV1, OtcSwapV1, RevokeV1, RedeemV1. Minting is proof of knowledge of a mint secret stored as token_auth_parent and bound into the asset id. Redemption burns the note and creates a zero-value receipt. The receipt is an on-chain redemption record, not proof of off-chain settlement.
  • Box and Purse are the ZK capability containers. Box is linear Put/Take: the old state is nullified and a new leaf is appended, so a capability can be delegated without a mutable account. Purse hides a balance in a Pedersen commitment and proves conservation homomorphically. The ZK versions are the fork-original work; the base abstractions are Agoric’s Invitation and ERTP Purse.
  • Identity, Oracle, Attestation are the optional credential and data layer. Identity issues credentials and proves predicates; Oracle proves who wrote a datum, not that it is true; Attestation generalizes claims and predicates. Their gates are issuer-level and opt-in.
  • Stablecoin and Bridge are issuer contracts. Stablecoin is a CDP over PN with a governance key and a dead-man switch; only the pooled-debt model is implemented. Bridge wraps an external asset as a PN 1:1; the wrapped note is user-held, external release is relayer-executed, and bridge-verify is fail-closed by default.
  • NativeToken and Uncle Merkle are the base layer. A shielded UTXO token with no premine; every block is checked against a Pedersen mass balance over native-token calls; the cumulative supply commitment chain is carried by the plaintext coinbase. This is host-side arithmetic, not a ZK block proof.
  • The wallet and manifests make it composable. Each contract with a manifest declares its functions, circuits, capability types, and actions in a TOML manifest. The wallet reads manifests, scans the chain, and composes capability types from the primitives it finds; keys stay in the AccountManager and never reach the daemon.
  • The assurance layer is explicit. Lean 4 models 32 zkVM opcodes and 10 gadget files; the verdict is mixed per opcode. There are 6 assumptions, with pallasPrime the only arithmetic one. The gate checks instance derivation for 167 contract circuits (179 walked); it does not prove circuit correctness. OBL-Z12 is closed for the known quotient circuits in the obligation register; the proof-status ledger still marks the general range-check discharge as partly open. Both artifacts are linked; where they differ, this scoped claim is the one stated here. Counts drift.
Method

Specify the failure mode before the architecture; prove what can be proved; contain what cannot; record provenance with every result. Where proof stops is stated in the limits.

4Provenance

DarkWow is a fork of DarkFi. The zkVM, ZKAS circuit language, P2P stack, WASM runtime, and Halo2 backend are inherited. The fork-original work is the object-capability governance layer and genesis set, Uncle Merkle consensus, the TOML manifest/ABI, the capability wallet architecture, the ZK Box/Purse and the contract suite, the supply audit, and the added opcodes. The code is AGPL-3.0.

The contract design is a direct descendant of the Rholang/RChain work: a name is authority, capabilities compose, and the constraints have to be enforced from the beginning. The Rholo port is the other side of that lineage; the lessons of what happens when those constraints are not enforced are part of why this contract set is ZK-only.

The fork-era work was built by one primary developer with AI as a co-architect. The architecture is designed to contain what generated code can reach: capability boundaries, deterministic tests, and manifest-first integration. The formal record is in the obligation register.

The design stands on the 2005 ρ-calculus paper (Meredith & Radestock), the 2020 Halo 2 release (Zcash NU5 2022), and the 2026 fork-era work. Dates are publication and release dates, not claims of first use.

6Boundaries

  • Issuer discretion. PN does not enforce redemption, collateral, or a supply cap. The issuer may refuse or dilute; the holder prices that as counterparty risk. A receipt is an on-chain redemption record, not proof of off-chain settlement.
  • The bridge is deferred by default. With bridge-verify off, every deposit is refused. With it on, Monero DLEq and Litecoin Merkle proofs verify; Litecoin MWEB range proofs are deferred; Ethereum, Zcash, and Aztec are fail-closed or stubbed (Ethereum has no trie traversal, receipt binding, or receipts-root check — OBL-C21). The wrapped note is user-held; external release is relayer-executed.
  • Contract-level gates are explicit counterparties. Identity issuers can revoke credentials; Stablecoin has a governance key and a dead-man switch; relayers can censor. These are not protocol-level freezes over the note.
  • The assurance boundary is written down. Lean covers opcodes and gadgets, not the contract circuits; 6 assumptions remain. OBL-Z12 is closed for the known quotient circuits in the obligation register, while the proof-status ledger still marks the general case partly open; one validator defect (OBL-C279) is deferred to the next release. The obligation register and proof-status ledger are both linked; where they differ, this scoped claim is the one stated here.
  • Known defects. The mass-balance check was inert until a token-commit constant was fixed, and historical blocks are unchecked. The obligation register records the current status; the check is host-side arithmetic, not a ZK block proof.
  • No protocol-level freeze, not no trust. The note itself cannot be frozen or confiscated by a protocol administrator. The issuer, the credential issuer, the relayer, and the external chain are explicit counterparties. That is the design, not a missing feature.
  • Manifests and wallet. Manifests are declarations, not an on-chain hash: seven of the nine genesis manifests travel in the genesis payload, post-genesis manifests are wallet-side, and Deployooor has none. The shipping wallet implements a subset of the ρ-calculus specification; there is no third-party audit and mainnet is TBD.
  • What stands between this and production. A third-party audit; bridge verifiers; the deferred validator defect OBL-C279. Mainnet is TBD.
