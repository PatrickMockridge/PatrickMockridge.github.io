Booking primitives on Rholo

R-Share

On-chain booking and rental primitives for Rholo, settled through R-Cash: a listing owns units, a unit owns slots, and a booking holds capacity over an interval with escrow, cancellation, deposits, disputes, and tickets.

What
Booking and rental primitives: a provider publishes a listing, the listing owns units, each unit owns slots, and a booking acquires capacity over an interval. Escrow, cancellation, deposits, disputes, and tickets are shared. Thirteen deploy terms; one allocation cell per listing; one booking cell and one bond per booking; R-Cash accounts and purses for settlement.
Why
A rental is a multi-party state machine with money in the middle. A database and a payment integration can run one business; they do not give two parties a shared ordering, atomic multi-slot acquisition, verifiable refund arithmetic, or escrow neither controls alone. Put the bookable state and the money on one chain and the application becomes a client, not a backend. The chain is the backend.
The combination
A small primitive set — capacity, interval, escrow, policy — with Rholo’s object capabilities, R-Cash settlement, and a per-listing allocator whose atomic acquisition is proved in Lean. The same path carries holiday letting, events, ticketing, bike share, car rental, and robotics-as-a-service; the product and the off-chain facts stay in the fork.

Method

Specify the failure mode before the architecture; prove what can be proved; contain what cannot; record provenance with every result. Where proof stops is stated in the limits.

1The model

A rental is capacity over an interval. A provider publishes a listing. The listing owns units. Each unit owns slots, and a slot is a record in the listing’s allocation cell, not a separate contract. A booking asks for one or more slots; if every requested slot is free or expired-held, the allocator writes them all in one commit and creates the booking.

That shape carries the lifecycle: terms, hold, escrow, confirm, check-in, check-out, settle. Every booking has its own state cell, ticket projection, operation map, and Bond holding the escrow purse. Cancellation, no-show, deposit claims, disputes, and arbitration are arms on the shared path. Settlement is through R-Cash accounts and purses.

The model is small on purpose. Capacity, interval, escrow, and policy are the primitives. Calendar, identity provider, support desk, insurance, telemetry, and regulation are the fork’s product.

2The ladder

The ladder is the design argument. Each rung adds a real-world constraint; the question is whether listing → unit → slot → booking still fits. A new rung changes kind, attrs, slot IDs, and at most one pure Remedy branch. It does not change the booking machine, escrow, settlement buckets, or authority model. If it needs a new booking arm, the primitive is wrong, or the requirement is a new primitive.

3Concurrency and the allocator

The doctrine is one sentence: make the tuple space do the arbitration, not a lock or a global cell.

  • One allocation cell per listing. Units and slots live in listingState; a slot is data, not a contract. No slot URI or live capability is published.
  • One commit. acquire validates the whole request and writes every requested slot in one take/commit. There is no reachable partial state.
  • Peek is a hint, never a lock. A stale peek causes a clean refusal, not a double booking.
  • select is forbidden. The node desugars PChoice into parallel receives, so it is not committed choice. Exclusivity comes from consuming one datum.
  • No global counters. Booking, listing, unit, and slot IDs are client- or provider-supplied and uniqueness-checked.
  • Per-listing serialization is accepted. Different listings never share a cell; different slots of one listing serialize. That is the price of atomic multi-slot acquisition, and the repository names it as the hotspot.

Lean proves the cornerstone: RShare.Model.Allocator.acquire_no_double_owner. After a successful acquire, every active reservation containing a requested key belongs to the acquiring booking. acquire_none_or_reservation and acquire_is_one_reservation state the shape: refuse and leave state unchanged, or append exactly one reservation. The proof is over the model, not a Rholang refinement.

4The client, and the fork

The repository is a community case study, not a product manual. It ships the contracts, a Rust SDK, a CLI, a WASM wallet, and a React reference client. There is no backend server, indexer, or off-chain database. The client uses a curated catalogue and share links; it has no provider inbox, search, notifications, or fiat ramp. The chain is the backend.

The client campaign covers the same surface from three directions. The CLI journey covers quote, book, held-slot refusal, replay, wrong-terms refusal, manual accept and cancel, one-signature refusal, and two-signature pause and resume. Browser journeys cover guest, provider, and admin-multisig. Conservation runs seven real settlement scenarios over a closed set of guest, provider, platform, and Bond balances, with a negative control that must detect untracked escrow.

Fork it. Keep the AGPL notices, copy contracts, harness, tools, crates, and web, change kind, attrs, slot IDs, terms, and at most one pure Remedy branch. The off-chain parts — identity, calendar, telemetry, support, insurance, regulation — are yours. R-Share is the settlement core; R-Cash is the money; neither is a marketplace.

5Evidence

On 3 November 2026, a fresh devnet built from Rholo e2d073d with R-Cash 49b441d recorded 16 passing suites: root, payments, lifecycle, claims, pricing, guestbook, booking, pause, allocator, admin, events, generality, provider, listing, behaviour, and conservation. Claim coverage is 20 of 30 cells; the four claim classes run across five kinds.

The allocator suite proves atomic multi-slot acquisition, token-only confirm and release, pause gates, expiry takeover, and the absence of a slot-capability leak. Conservation covers seven real settlement scenarios with a closed balance set and a negative control. The Lean model records 37 axiom audits, 21 vectors, 33 rules, 29 arms, and 13 core arms proved-model.

6Limits

  • Maturity. Devnet-only, unaudited, pinned to R-Cash v0.5 at 49b441d; no real value.
  • Allocation. Per-listing serialization is the accepted hotspot. Different listings do not share a cell; different slots of one listing serialize.
  • Privacy. Bookings, addresses, dates, and prices are on-chain. There is no confidential booking and no KYC, legal identity, or reputation in the core.
  • Unrun breadth. providerDeliveryBreach and partialPerformance have no code. Fleet-scale allocation, station capacity, provider-bond legs, obligation objects, generalized claims, and evidence enforcement are not run.
  • Out of scope. Backend, indexer, off-chain database, cross-shard settlement, telemetry, remote stop, machine safety, and a full governor or timelock.
  • Verification boundary. The 37 axiom audits are an internal Lean audit, not a security audit. Lean is model-level, not a Rholang refinement.
  • Governance. No code of conduct, security-disclosure policy, or maintainer council is in place.
