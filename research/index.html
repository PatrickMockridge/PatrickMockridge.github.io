Research & Writing

A critical risk-analysis framework, applied through simulation and public data.

Two questions start this work, and neither is an engineering question: why coordinated systems fail under information asymmetry, and how influence, secrets, and compromise move through a network of peers. The Romeo–Juliet Framework is the general risk-analysis layer; UNISS is the simulation layer.

UNISS did not begin as a simulation. It grew out of a live security dispute and an evidence-archiving effort: first the archive, then the question of how influence, secrecy, and compromise move through a network. The simulator models that system rather than narrating it.

1Romeo–Juliet framework

The Romeo–Juliet Framework is a critical risk-analysis framework for any coordinated system operating under information asymmetry: AI, blockchains, politics, business, engineering. It maps the “deathly embrace” between problem-solvers and the capital order onto four squares — preparation (green), action (yellow), access (red), and status (blue) — and models the actors and sequestration channels that determine whether a project can move from zero to one.

The actors — Oscar, Mallory, Eve, Sybil, Olivia, and Walter — and the three sequestration channels (diagonal, left, right) describe how value is captured, how access is blocked, and where a project can move. The framework draws on Schumpeter, Kondratiev, Henry George, Gambetta, Nitzan and Bichler, Thiel, Nash, and the ρ-calculus/process-calculus tradition.

It is a critical risk-analysis tool first: it reads the failure modes of a system before the architecture is fixed, not after the fact. The paper applies the same structure to centralised AI, state power, intelligence agencies, rent collection, voting, law, engineering, and business. The CoEngineers experience (2017–2019), where I was an early investor, was one origin of the framework, not its scope.

2UNISS / Ingenesist

UNISS is the simulation layer: containment before a system exists — what moves, who can see it, and where a boundary has to sit. It models how influence, secrets, and layered compromise move through networked communities. The series extends through ESN, GX, GX-SD, and Ingenesist scenarios, including the Darkweave-Ingenesist endowment model.

The Ingenesist archive preserves Dan Roble’s presentations and papers published with NAIC, ASME, ASCE, NSPE, and others. The “blockchain for engineers” line is the lineage for UNISS. Ingenesist archive.

3NFL / Big Data Bowl 2027

Big Data Bowl entry. A judged entry to the 2027 NFL Big Data Bowl. A 10 Hz Combine burst of speed and acceleration predicts interior pressure and sacks; the 40-yard dash and 10-yard split do not. The signal is position-specific — real on the defensive line, marginal at receiver, absent on the offensive line and at tight end. The entry’s second finding is an allocation gap: the cap pays for performance, but the depth chart follows prior snaps and draft position, not measured performance. The preregistered RJF/Yellow Square test found no detectable effect and is reported as a perspective, not confirmation.

The dollar findings. The follow-on valuation prices that gap. On the actual 2026 free-agent class, the median team carries about $7.0M a year of role premium — pay that tracks playing time beyond measured performance — or $6.0M net after a $1M implementation cost. The role premium is +0.179 log points on 2026 APY (95% CI 0.022–0.336, n=306), close to +0.185 on the 2025 cap ledger. The team distribution runs from $1.8M at P10 to $22.5M at P90, while the conservative EDGE/safety/corner case is $1.4M gross ($0.4M net); the top 10% of contracts carry 51% of the premium. These are inferences from the 2026 class, not forecasts or causal estimates.
